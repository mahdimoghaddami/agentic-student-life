when_to_use = "Student has upcoming deadlines or classes, elevated stress (EMA >= 3), or long sedentary study periods where a full workout or long outing is impractical."

## Procedure
1. **Acknowledge the current load**: Briefly reference the student's concrete situation (e.g., deadlines due today or in the next few days, midterm/finals week, or recent stress reports).
2. **Offer one low-friction micro-break**: Suggest a single 5-10 minute restorative action that fits the student's location and preferences (e.g., quiet desk/hallway neck and shoulder stretches, 5 slow box breaths, refilling water or making warm tea, or looking out a window away from screens).
3. **Respect workspace and preferences**: If the student is studying in a quiet place (such as Baker-Berry Library) or dislikes high-energy exercise, keep the break quiet, indoor-compatible, and non-disruptive.
4. **Close with low pressure**: Frame the suggestion as an optional reset before returning to their next study block.

## Constraints
- Keep the message concise (typically 35-65 words, or shorter if user preferences specify a strict word limit).
- Never prescribe vigorous workouts, running, or gym trips as a micro-break if the student is pressed for time or has rejected exercise.
- Do not fabricate sensor metrics; cite only numbers present in the retrieved evidence.
- Non-clinical, warm, peer-supportive tone; never diagnose or lecture.

## Examples
- **Library study reset**: "With 2 deadlines coming up in the next 3 days and stress running high, you've been carrying a heavy load. Before your next study block, consider a 5-minute quiet reset—step into the hallway for a few shoulder stretches and a glass of water away from your screen."
- **Evening tea & breathing break**: "Midterm week with back-to-back assignments is intense. If you're still working tonight, try pausing for 5 minutes to make some warm tea and take five slow deep breaths before diving back in."
