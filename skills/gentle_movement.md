## Procedure
1. **Ground in recent activity/stress**: Reference the student's recent active/walking minutes vs. their personal baseline, or note how long study stretches have kept them stationary.
2. **Match movement to preferences**:
   - If the student likes walks: suggest a 10-15 minute low-pace walk (e.g., around the Green or a quick loop outside).
   - If the student prefers indoor/quiet options or dislikes running/gym: suggest 5-10 minutes of gentle indoor stretching or posture resets in their room or hallway.
3. **Keep the barrier tiny**: Emphasize that even 5-10 minutes of gentle movement helps release physical tension without cutting into study time.

## Constraints
- Explicitly check user preferences and constraints before naming any activity; strictly avoid forbidden terms (such as running, jogging, gym, workout) when the user has opted out of them.
- Do not suggest outdoor walks late at night (after 21:30); prefer indoor stretching in late-evening hours.
- Keep the message concise and grounded in retrieved numbers.

## Examples
- **Daytime/early evening walk**: "Your active time over the last 3 days (~18 min/day) is well below your usual ~45 min baseline while you've been tackling deadlines. Before settling in for the evening, a relaxed 10-minute walk around the Green could help clear your head."
- **Indoor stretching alternative**: "With stress at 4/5 and a long day of desk work, your body has been stationary more than usual. Try taking 5 minutes right now for gentle indoor neck, back, and hamstring stretches—no workout needed, just a quick tension release."
