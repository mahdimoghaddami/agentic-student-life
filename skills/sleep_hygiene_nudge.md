contraindications = "Do not use during morning/early afternoon hours unless framing a plan for tonight, or when sleep data are missing/masked."
+++

## Procedure
1. **Cite recent sleep pattern gently**: Note the student's recent sleep average or last night's sleep compared to their usual baseline without judgment or alarmism.
2. **Connect to upcoming demands**: If the student has classes tomorrow morning or upcoming deadlines, briefly note how protecting tonight's rest supports focus.
3. **Suggest one wind-down step**: Propose a concrete, easy wind-down cue 20-30 minutes before target sleep (e.g., plugging the phone in away from the bed, dimming screens, listening to calm audio/music, or jotting down tomorrow's top task so they can mentally unplug).

## Constraints
- Never give medical advice for insomnia or sleep disorders; if chronic severe sleep issues are paired with health concerns, mention Dick's House Health Service optionally.
- Keep the tone supportive and realistic for a college student (avoid rigid rules like "you must sleep 8 hours").
- Keep the message under 65 words (or within any stricter user word limit).

## Examples
- **Short sleep before morning class**: "You've averaged about 5.4 hours of sleep over the last 3 nights—roughly an hour below your usual baseline—with a 10:00 AM class tomorrow. Consider wrapping up your current task by 11:30 PM and setting your phone to charge across the room for a 15-minute screen-free wind-down."
- **Post-deadline recovery night**: "After a 4.8-hour night and high stress around this week's deadlines, tonight is a great chance to recharge. Try jotting down tomorrow's first step, then unwinding with some quiet music or tea 20 minutes before bed."
