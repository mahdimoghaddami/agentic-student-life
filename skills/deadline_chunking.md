when_to_use = "Student has >= 1 deadline today or >= 2 deadlines in the next 3 days (especially paired with EMA stress >= 4) and needs structure to overcome overwhelm."
contraindications = "Do not use when the student has 0 deadlines in the next 3 days and low stress."
+++

## Procedure
1. **Validate the deadline cluster**: Cite the exact number of deadlines due today and over the next 3 days (and courses if relevant) so the student feels understood.
2. **Propose a single time-boxed chunk**: Suggest picking just one priority deliverable and working on a small, well-defined sub-task for 25-30 minutes (e.g., outlining one section, solving one problem, or reviewing lecture notes), followed by a 5-minute break.
3. **Mention academic support if helpful**: Optionally note the Academic Skills Center or Baker-Berry Library study spaces if the student could benefit from structured study support.

## Constraints
- Do not invent assignment names beyond the course codes and deadline counts in the academic tool output.
- Keep the tone calm and practical; avoid adding pressure or long multi-step checklists.
- Respect any user preferences regarding break styles and word length.

## Examples
- **Clustered deadlines + high stress**: "You have 1 deadline today and 2 more over the next 3 days, and your recent stress reports (4/5) show how heavy that feels. Instead of tackling everything at once, pick just the most urgent item for one 25-minute focused block, then take a 5-minute stretch or tea break."
- **Midterm week pacing**: "With 3 deadlines coming up in the next 3 days across your courses, it helps to shrink the target: spend 10 minutes listing the single next step for each assignment, tackle just one for 30 minutes, and pause to reset."
